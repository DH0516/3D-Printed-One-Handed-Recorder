# 6. Troubleshooting

### 6.1 Sound & Acoustic Issues

- **Issue: The recorder sounds "airy", weak, or fails to speak on low notes (Low C, C#, D).**
  
  - **Cause 1: Inadequate Key Pad Seal.** Inspect each key pad using a flashlight placed inside the bore in a darkened room or test with a narrow paper feeler gauge. If light leaks around the pad rim, re-seat or sand the tone hole chimney flat.
  - **Cause 2: Tenon Joint Leakage.** Air escaping at the head-to-body or body-to-foot joint weakens acoustic pressure. Add extra layers of thread, PTFE tape, or cork wrapping to achieve an airtight seal.
  - **Cause 3: Leaky Print Walls.** If improperly printed, air can bleed through micro-gaps between layer lines, usually happens around toneholes. Ensure at least 4 perimeter walls were used, or seal the interior bore with a thin coat of mineral oil or paraffin wax. Sparse Infill patterns likely will not change the acoustic outcome.

- **Issue: Lower register sounds weak compared to upper, or constantly overblows.**
  
  - **Cause 1: Octave Thumb Hole or Key Leak.** The thumb hole key (Key 1) or other keyed hole must seal completely when fully closed and provide a clean aperture when vented.
  - **Cause 2: Leaky Print Walls.**

### 6.2 Mechanical & Keywork Issues

- **Issue: Keys stick, bind, or are sluggish to return.**
  
  - **Cause 1: Pivot Friction.** The pivot hole in the key hinge may be too tight on the axle pin. Gently sand around the hole, or the key insert.
  - **Cause 2: Insufficient Spring Tension.** Increase preload on the torsion spring or use a slightly shorter/tighter elastic band.
  - **Cause 3: Key Arm Clearance.** Check that key arms do not rub against adjacent pillar posts or the outer curvature of the body.

- **Issue: Key pads fall off or shift out of alignment.**
  
  - **Solution:** Clean the key cup thoroughly with isopropyl alcohol before gluing. Use gel cyanoacrylate or contact cement, ensuring full surface contact and allowing adequate cure time under clamping pressure.

### 6.3 Joint Fit & Physical Tolerances

- **Issue: Joints are too tight to assemble or bind during insertion.**
  
  - **Solution:** Do not force printed joints together. Apply less thread/cork wrapping. If the bare printed tenon is oversized, wet-sand the outer diameter with 400-grit sandpaper until it inserts smoothly. Then apply cork grease or wax.

- **Issue: Joints are loose or wobble during play.**
  
  - **Solution:** Add more tenon wrap until the connection is snug, rigid, and airtight.
