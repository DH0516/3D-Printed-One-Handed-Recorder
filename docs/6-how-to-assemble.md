# 5. How to Assemble

The design requires **zero screws** and **no bought metal springs**. Key brackets snap directly into integrated pillar slots on the body, and standard 1.75 mm printer filament serves both as the pivot pin for Key 1 and as the pin springs for Keys 2 to 4. Follow the steps in order.

### Step 1: Clean and Inspect Printed Parts

- Remove all slicer support material from the keys.
- Check the internal bore of the body and foot joint. Clear away any strings, burrs, or loose filament bits so the air column is unobstructed.
- Inspect each tone hole rim. Ensure the rims are clean, flat, and free of burrs; lightly touch them with fine sandpaper if needed so the key pads seat completely flat.
- Lightly sand the male tenons and female sockets with 400-grit sandpaper until parts test-fit smoothly without binding.
- Optionally apply a thin coat of food-grade oil (flax, or similar used in woodwind oiling) to the internal bore for improved sealing.

### Step 2: Prepare and Glue Sealing Pads

- Punch or cut circular pad discs (1.0-1.5 mm closed-cell neoprene, natural cork, or similar) matching the key cup diameter.
- Place a small drop of gel cyanoacrylate or craft adhesive into each pad cup, then press the pad disc firmly into place, making sure it sits flat and level without tilting.
- Let the glue cure fully before mounting the keys.

### Step 3: Mount Keys 2 to 4

- Test-fit each printed key arm into its corresponding pivot pillar on the recorder body.
- Align the key bracket with the open slots in the body pillars and gently press it down until it snaps securely into place.
- Test each key: it should turn smoothly on its pivot without binding or excessive wobble.

### Step 4: Install the Filament Pin Springs (Keys 2 to 4)

- Cut a short, straight snippet of standard 1.75 mm filament for each key.
- Insert a standard 1.75 mm 3D printing FDM filament (or applicable pins) through the pillar posts and pin holes; it acts as a pin spring.
- Rotate the filament pin in the direction that applies its appropriate sealing force.
- Snap off the filament at the notch so the pin spring stays in one rotational position.
- Press each touchpiece with your finger: the pad should lift smoothly, and when you release, the pin spring should snap the pad shut instantly over the tone hole rim.

### Step 5: Mount Key 1 (Octave Lever)

- Position Key 1 into its pin slot on the body.
- Cut a straight snippet of 1.75 mm filament to the width of the pin slot and slide it through the pin slot holes and Key 1, creating a clean pivot pin. Trim any excess flush with the pin slot walls.
- Key 1's return spring is the flexible printed sheet spring, printed as part of the key itself; it keeps the octave hole closed at rest until the touchpiece is pressed.

### Step 6: Attach the Foot Joint

- Wrap the body's lower tenon with paper tape, thread, an O-ring, or cork (all work fine) to establish an airtight friction fit.
- Align the foot joint with the body and press it on firmly.

### Step 7: Mate with the Head Joint

- Wrap the body's upper tenon with paper tape, thread, an O-ring, or cork until a firm, airtight friction fit is achieved. Apply a light touch of cork grease if needed.
- Push the body firmly into the commercial soprano head joint.
- Visually align the front tone holes with the labium window on the head joint so the instrument is straight.

### Step 8: Perform Airtightness and Playability Checks

- Gently blow warm air through the recorder while keeping all tone holes closed.
- Listen and feel for air leaks around the pad cups. If a pad is not sealing completely, check that the pad sits flat in its cup and that the pin spring provides enough closing force.
- Test finger action: press each touchpiece to ensure full, unobstructed travel and snappy spring return.
- Play a chromatic scale following the fingering chart.

### Design Customizations for Tenons

- **Grooves for Threads:** If enough users request it, the author will add versions of the model files with pre-cut grooves designed specifically for thread wrapping. Note that this is not a standard design for recorders.
- **O-Ring Grooves:** Some have suggested O-ring seals. If there is sufficient interest, there will be a version featuring O-ring groove joints.
- **Modifying & Sharing:** You are welcome to download the design files and edit them to fit your specific needs. If you make modifications and wish to share them with the community for this instrument, send your designs to the author to be posted on the project site.
