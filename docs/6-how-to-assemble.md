# 5. How to Assemble

1. **Preparing the Body & Tone Holes:** 
   - Inspect the printed bore and tone holes for any stringing, burrs, or layer seam bumps.
   - Clean tone hole perimeters using fine needle files or 600-grit sandpaper, ensuring smooth seating rims for key pads.
   - Lightly sand the male tenons and female sockets with 400-grit sandpaper until parts test-fit smoothly without binding.
   - Optionally apply a thin coat of food-grade mineral oil or melted paraffin wax to the internal bore for improved acoustic resonance and moisture resistance.

2. **Mounting the Keywork:** 
   - Test-fit each printed key arm into its corresponding pivot pillar/post on the recorder body.
   - Insert 1.0 mm or 1.2 mm stainless steel wire (or brass pivot pins) through the pillar posts and key hinges.
   - Verify that all keys pivot freely with zero friction and minimal lateral play.
   - Attach small stainless steel torsion springs or silicone/orthodontic elastic bands between the key anchors and body posts, confirming that each key snaps briskly back to its resting position upon release.

3. **Applying & Checking Pad Seals:** 
   - Punch or cut circular pad discs (1.0–1.5 mm closed-cell neoprene, instrument leather, or natural cork) matching the key cup diameter.
   - Apply a small drop of gel cyanoacrylate (super glue) or contact cement inside the key cup, then press the pad disc firmly into place.
   - Once dry, check seal airtightness: gently press the key closed over its tone hole and perform a suction test (blocking one end of the joint and drawing air) to ensure an airtight seal with no hiss or leakage.

4. **Final Joint Fitting & Testing:** 
   - Wrap the tenon joints with cork sheet, thread, or PTFE tape as detailed below until a firm, airtight friction fit is achieved.
   - Apply a light touch of cork grease or wax lubricant.
   - Align the commercial soprano head joint with the body tone holes, insert the foot joint, and test play the full chromatic scale using the fingering chart.

## 5.1 Tenon Joints & Sealing
To connect the instrument joints (head, middle, and foot) with an airtight, stable friction fit, you must wrap the tenons (the connecting joints). 

### Sealing Materials:
- **Cork (Modern Standard):** Newer commercially manufactured instruments often use cork sheets. Cork is chosen because it is highly compressible, moisture-resistant, and maintains a durable, uniform seal over time with minimal maintenance.
- **Thread (Historical Standard):** Historically, woodwinds used wrapped threads (such as silk, cotton, or synthetic threads) to build up a custom friction seal. All of these thread types should work.
- **Quick Alternatives:** Standard PTFE (Teflon) tape or dental floss can be used to quickly create an airtight joint. For a simple and immediate solution, the author personally uses paper tape.

### Design Customizations & Future Grooves:
- **Grooves for Threads:** If enough users request it, the author will add versions of the model files with pre-cut grooves designed specifically for thread wrapping.
- **O-Ring Grooves:** Many users have suggested O-ring seals. If there is sufficient interest, the author will release a version of the files featuring O-ring groove joints.
- **Modifying & Sharing:** You are welcome to download the design files and edit them to fit your specific needs. If you make modifications and wish to share them with the community for this instrument, send your designs to the author to be posted on the project site.
